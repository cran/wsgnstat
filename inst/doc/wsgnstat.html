wsgnstat: the wrapped two-piece exponential power distribution

library(wsgnstat)

Introduction

The two-piece exponential power distribution joins two halves of an exponential power density with different scales at a location \(\mu\). It goes back to the construction of Fernández and Steel (1998), and it has appeared under several names since. The package name keeps the authors’ earlier one, the (wrapped) skew generalised normal distribution. This vignette treats the distribution and its wrapped version on the circle as a uniform scale mixture. Section 2 gives the mixture and checks it by simulation, Section 3 the characteristic function and the trigonometric moments for small and large shapes, and Section 4 the accuracy of the wrapped density. Section 5 fits the four-parameter wrapped distribution to wind directions under a lower bound on the shape, and Section 6 fits rounded data by the grouped likelihood.

2. The distribution as a uniform scale mixture

Let \(\mu\) be real, \(\sigma > 0\), \(0 < \lambda < 1\) and \(\beta > 0\), and write \(a = \lambda/\sigma\) and \(b = (1 - \lambda)/\sigma\) for the rates to the right and to the left of \(\mu\). Let \(Z > 0\) be such that \(Z^\beta\) has the gamma distribution with shape \(1 + 1/\beta\), let \(V\) be uniform on \([-1/b, 1/a]\), independent of \(Z\), and put \(X = \mu + ZV\). Given \(Z\), \(X\) is uniform on the interval \([\mu - Z/b, \mu + Z/a]\), which contains \(\mu\), and \(X\) has the density \[ f(x) = c\exp\{-(a(x - \mu))^\beta\},\quad x \ge \mu, \qquad f(x) = c\exp\{-(b(\mu - x))^\beta\},\quad x < \mu, \] where \(c = \lambda(1 - \lambda)/\{\sigma\Gamma(1 + 1/\beta)\}\). The value \(\lambda = 1/2\) gives the symmetric exponential power distribution, \(\beta = 2\) with \(\lambda = 1/2\) the normal distribution with standard deviation \(\sqrt{2}\sigma\), and \(\beta = 1\) the asymmetric Laplace distribution. The mixture also gives \(\mathrm{P}(X < \mu) = \lambda\) and the moments \(\mathrm{E}(X - \mu)^k = \mathrm{E}Z^k\,\mathrm{E}V^k\) without splitting at \(\mu\). In the package the line functions take the right and left rate parameters as lam1 = lam and lam2 = 1 - lam.

rsgn() draws from the mixture with a single uniform variable. A Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between \(2 \times 10^4\) draws and the closed-form distribution function psgn() checks it, at a shape below one, where the density has a cusp at \(\mu\), and at a shape above two.

set.seed(1)
ks <- function(u) {
  u <- sort(u)
  n <- length(u)
  max(pmax(seq_len(n) / n - u, u - (seq_len(n) - 1) / n))
}
x1 <- rsgn(2e4, mu = 0, sigma = 0.4, beta = 0.3, lam1 = 0.3, lam2 = 0.7)
x2 <- rsgn(2e4, mu = 0, sigma = 1, beta = 2.5, lam1 = 0.7, lam2 = 0.3)
d_ks <- c(ks(psgn(x1, 0, 0.4, 0.3, 0.3, 0.7)), ks(psgn(x2, 0, 1, 2.5, 0.7, 0.3)))
left <- c(mean(x1 < 0), mean(x2 < 0))
mom <- function(k, s, be, l) {
  a <- l / s
  b <- (1 - l) / s
  gamma(1 + (k + 1) / be) / gamma(1 + 1 / be) * (a^-(k + 1) - (-b)^-(k + 1)) /
    ((k + 1) * (1 / a + 1 / b))
}
rbind(KS_distance = d_ks, below_mu = left, lam = c(0.3, 0.7),
      mean = c(mean(x1), mean(x2)), E_X = c(mom(1, 0.4, 0.3, 0.3), mom(1, 1, 2.5, 0.7)))

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov distances, 0.0055 and 0.0048, are below the 0.1 per cent critical value 0.0138. The fractions of draws below \(\mu\) are within Monte Carlo error of \(\lambda\), and the sample means of the moment formula. The mean of the heavy-tailed member is noisy, since its variance is large.

The wrapped distribution is that of \(\Theta = X \bmod 2\pi\). Given \(Z\), \(\Theta\) is uniform on an arc of length \(Z(1/a + 1/b)\) that contains \(\mu\), and its density is the image sum \[ g(\theta) = c\sum_{k \ge 0}\left[\exp\{-(a(t + 2\pi k))^\beta\} + \exp\{-(b(2\pi - t + 2\pi k))^\beta\}\right], \qquad t = (\theta - \mu) \bmod 2\pi . \] dwsgn(), pwsgn(), qwsgn() and rwsgn() take the four parameters mu, sigma, lam and beta. The four-parameter wrapped distribution is identifiable. When sigma is omitted, the reciprocal-rate subfamily \(ab = 1\), that is \(\sigma = \{\lambda(1 - \lambda)\}^{1/2}\), is used, as in version 0.1.0; it is a three-parameter restriction of the model. Figure 1 shows how \(\beta\) moves the peak from a cusp to a flat top and how \(\lambda\) tilts the mass about \(\mu\).

3. Characteristic function and trigonometric moments

From the mixture, the characteristic function is \[ \varphi(t) = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mu}\,c\left\{\frac{1}{a}G\!\left(\frac{t}{a}\right) + \frac{1}{b}\overline{G\!\left(\frac{t}{b}\right)}\right\},\qquad G(w) = \int_0^\infty \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}wu - u^\beta}\,\mathrm{d}u, \] for every \(\beta > 0\), and the trigonometric moments of \(\Theta\) are \(\varphi_p = \varphi(p)\). On the real axis the integral oscillates, and for small shapes its integrand decays so slowly that it has to be followed out to \(37^{1/\beta}\), about \(5 \times 10^{15}\) at \(\beta = 0.1\). Version 0.1.0 applied Simpson’s rule there, which failed below \(\beta = 0.6\). Since \(\mathrm{e}^{-u^\beta}\) is analytic off the negative axis, the path of integration may be turned onto the ray \(u = r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi}\) for any \(0 \le \phi < \min\{\pi, \pi/(2\beta)\}\), along which both factors decay and the oscillation is slow. With \(r = \mathrm{e}^x\) the integrand is analytic in a strip about the real \(x\)-axis, so the trapezoid rule converges exponentially as the step is halved, and cfsgn() stops when two successive sums agree to tol (default \(10^{-12}\)). The convergent series, descending for \(\beta < 1\) and ascending for \(\beta > 1\), are available as method = "series" for checking.

The characteristic function at zero must be one. Version 0.1.0 returned 40624 at \(\beta = 0.1\).

shapes <- c(0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 1, 2, 3)
cf0 <- vapply(shapes, function(b) Mod(cfsgn(0, 0, 0.4, b, 0.3, 0.7) - 1), 0)
setNames(cf0, paste0("beta=", shapes))
#> beta=0.1 beta=0.2 beta=0.3 beta=0.5   beta=1   beta=2   beta=3 
#>        0        0        0        0        0        0        0

Below shape one the descending series converges, and it agrees with the rotated-path quadrature at small and large arguments. At \(\beta = 1\) and at \(\beta = 2\) with \(\lambda = 1/2\) the characteristic function has the closed forms \(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mu}ab/\{(a - \mathrm{i}t)(b + \mathrm{i}t)\}\) and \(\exp(\mathrm{i}t\mu - \sigma^2t^2)\).

tt <- c(0.5, 1, 3, 10, 40)
small <- vapply(c(0.1, 0.3, 0.7), function(b) {
  max(Mod(cfsgn(tt, 0.3, 0.4, b, 0.3, 0.7) - cfsgn(tt, 0.3, 0.4, b, 0.3, 0.7, method = "series")))
}, 0)

The descending series also gives the exact decay of the trigonometric moments. Unless \(\lambda = 1/2\) and \(\beta\) is an even integer, \(|\varphi_p| = Cp^{-(1 + \beta)}\{1 + o(1)\}\), with \(C = c\,\Gamma(\beta + 1)\,|a^\beta\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi(\beta + 1)/2} + b^\beta\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi(\beta + 1)/2}|\), which trig_decay() returns. The decay is set by the singularity of the density at \(\mu\), so a skew member decays like \(p^{-(1 + \beta)}\) even at \(\beta = 2\).

p <- c(10, 100, 1000)
ratio <- vapply(c(0.3, 1.5, 2), function(be) {
  tm <- trig_moments(1000, 0, be, 0.3, sigma = 0.4)
  Mod(complex(real = tm$A[p], imaginary = tm$B[p])) * p^(1 + be) / trig_decay(be, 0.3, 0.4)["C"]
}, numeric(3))
dimnames(ratio) <- list(paste0("p=", p), paste0("beta=", c(0.3, 1.5, 2)))
round(ratio, 4)
#>        beta=0.3 beta=1.5 beta=2
#> p=10     0.6269   1.1590 1.3545
#> p=100    0.7913   1.0047 1.0022
#> p=1000   0.8893   1.0001 1.0000

The ratio \(|\varphi_p|p^{1 + \beta}/C\) tends to one in each column, slowly at \(\beta = 0.3\), where the relative remainder is of order \(p^{-\beta}\).

4. Accuracy of the wrapped density

For small shapes the terms of the image sum decay very slowly. Version 0.1.0 summed 50 images on each side and stopped, which left out most of the mass below \(\beta = 0.3\). dwsgn() now sums K images directly, or fewer when the rest is negligible. The remainder is added by the Euler-Maclaurin formula, with the exact incomplete-gamma integral and the terms in the first and third derivatives. Images are added directly until the first omitted term of the formula is negligible, so the result does not depend on K, and the density integrates to one even at \(\beta = 0.05\). The plain sum of 50 images, computed below from the line density, shows the size of the old truncation error.

th <- seq(0, 2 * pi, length.out = 25)[-25]
acc <- t(vapply(c(0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 1, 2), function(be) {
  d50 <- dwsgn(th, 0.7, be, 0.3, K = 50, sigma = 0.4)
  d3 <- dwsgn(th, 0.7, be, 0.3, K = 3, sigma = 0.4)
  plain <- rowSums(matrix(dsgn(outer(th, 2 * pi * (-50:50), "+"), 0.7, 0.4, be, 0.3, 0.7),
                          nrow = length(th)))
  tot <- stats::integrate(dwsgn, 0, 2 * pi, mu = 0.7, beta = be, lam = 0.3, sigma = 0.4,
                          rel.tol = 1e-12, subdivisions = 2000L)$value
  c(beta = be, K3_vs_K50 = max(abs(d3 / d50 - 1)), mass_minus_1 = tot - 1,
    plain50_rel_error = max(abs(plain / d50 - 1)))
}, numeric(4)))
signif(acc, 3)
#>      beta K3_vs_K50 mass_minus_1 plain50_rel_error

The package also carries image sums computed independently to 40 digits by mpmath’s Euler-Maclaurin summation, at six angles, two scales and shapes from 0.1 to 2.

The largest relative error is 2.2e-15, below \(10^{-14}\) on this grid of six angles, two scales, \(\lambda = 0.3\) and shapes 0.1 to 2. pwsgn() sums the exact incomplete-gamma probabilities of the images in the same way, so that pwsgn(2 * pi) is one, and qwsgn() inverts it.

5. The four-parameter fit with a shape floor

wind <- as.numeric(dat$wind)
t_wind <- system.time(fit <- fit_wsgn(wind, se = "none"))["elapsed"]
fit
#> Wrapped two-piece exponential power distribution, maximum likelihood  
#> n = 310  log-likelihood = -369.0648  shape floor = 0.1     
#>               mu  sigma    lam  beta
#> estimate 0.01852 0.0847 0.3085 0.571
#> se            NA     NA     NA    NA
#> standard errors: none

The data of the circular package wind are 310 wind directions recorded at Col de la Roa in the Italian Alps between 3 and 4 a.m. each day from 29 January to 31 March 2001 (Agostinelli, 2007). Readings 15 minutes apart are dependent, so the fit is descriptive.

With the shape free, the likelihood of the wrapped distribution is unbounded as \(\beta\) tends to zero, with \(\mu\) at an observation. At every fixed shape it is bounded, unless all observations coincide. Under a lower bound \(\beta \ge \beta_0\) that does not exceed the true shape, the maximum likelihood estimator is consistent. fit_wsgn() therefore maximises over \(\beta_0 \le \beta \le 20\), by default with \(\beta_0 = 0.1\). It searches both the regular branch and the spike branch, at three shapes, the floor times 1, 1.25 and 1.6, with the location at every observation, and returns the best; at_floor reports a fit on the floor and at_rate_bound a degenerate fit. The fitted shape, 0.57, is well above the floor.

When the fitted shape is at most one, the log-likelihood is convex in \(\mu\) between consecutive observations, so every maximising location is an observation. The location is then searched over the stored values near the fit, and the fitted location is indeed one of the observations (TRUE). At a fitted shape below one fit_wsgn() gives no standard errors by default, since asymptotic normality is proved only for shapes above one. A parametric bootstrap is available with se = "bootstrap", each refit repeating both searches and taking as long as the fit itself (52 seconds here), so it is left out of this vignette.

The profile likelihood \(M(\beta)\) shows whether the degenerate branch below the floor comes near the fit. As \(\beta \to 0\) it rises at the leading rate \(A^*/\beta\), with \(A^* = n\log\{n/(n - m)\}\) and \(m\) the largest number of tied observations.

pr <- profile_wsgn(wind, beta = c(0.3, unname(coef(fit)["beta"]), 1, 2), window = 0.4)
astar <- n_w * log(n_w / (n_w - m_w))
cbind(pr[, c("beta", "logLik")], A_over_beta = astar / pr$beta)
#>        beta    logLik A_over_beta
#> 1 0.3000000 -378.0811    6.688265
#> 2 0.5710469 -369.0648    3.513686
#> 3 1.0000000 -377.9621    2.006479
#> 4 2.0000000 -411.3535    1.003240

Here \(m = 2\) and \(A^* = 2.01\). The profile has its maximum at the fitted shape and falls away on both sides; at \(\beta = 0.3\) it is already 9.0 below the fitted value. With so small an \(A^*\) the degenerate branch plays no part at the shapes that matter. The profile at shapes near the floor is slow to compute for these data, since the image sums are long there, and is left out.

The von Mises distribution fitted by circular::mle.vonmises() is far behind in log-likelihood and AIC.

vm <- circular::mle.vonmises(circular::circular(wind))
ll_vm <- sum(log(circular::dvonmises(circular::circular(wind), vm$mu, vm$kappa)))
data.frame(model = c("wrapped two-piece", "von Mises"), k = c(4, 2),
           logLik = c(fit$logLik, ll_vm), AIC = c(AIC(fit), 4 - 2 * ll_vm))
#>               model k    logLik      AIC
#> 1 wrapped two-piece 4 -369.0648 746.1296

For a fitted shape clearly above one, fit_wsgn() gives standard errors from the expected Fisher information \(I(\hat\vartheta)^{-1}/n\), which is finite for \(\beta > 1/2\). A simulated sample shows this route.

fit_sim <- fit_wsgn(sim)
round(rbind(truth = c(1, 0.5, 0.3, 1.8), estimate = coef(fit_sim), se = fit_sim$se), 3)

Speed

A fit is a search from many starting values, a screen of candidate locations and, for the smallest shapes, a spike search, and these trials are independent of one another. Where the package is built with OpenMP, ncores runs them on several threads, and profile_wsgn() takes the same argument. The best trial is chosen afterwards in a fixed order, so the answer is identical, to the last bit, for every number of threads; ncores = 1 is the default. A bootstrap spreads its refits over the threads instead.

sim40 <- sim[1:40]
f_serial <- fit_wsgn(sim40, se = "none")
f_two <- fit_wsgn(sim40, se = "none", ncores = 2)
identical(coef(f_serial), coef(f_two))
#> [1] TRUE

A fit takes minutes from about a thousand observations on, so threads pay there. A likelihood evaluation with 128 or more observations (or cells) is itself split over the threads and summed in a fixed order. On a laptop with four performance and six efficiency cores, busy with other work, a fit of 1500 observations took 160 seconds on one thread, 86 on two, 57 on four and 55 on eight, and one of 400 observations 36, 18, 11 and 13 seconds. That is a factor of about 2.8 on four threads, and little more beyond, because some of the work still runs in sequence (the first pass of the location search, the Nelder-Mead runs and the final polish) and because the extra threads of such a machine run on its slower cores.

6. The grouped likelihood for rounded data

Circular data are usually recorded to a resolution, and ties then make the degenerate branch visible at shapes that look ordinary. The ants of Fisher (1993, data set B.7, fisherB7 in circular) are 100 directions recorded as multiples of 10 degrees.

ants <- as.numeric(dat$fisherB7) * pi / 180
m_a <- max(table(dat$fisherB7))
astar_a <- 100 * log(100 / (100 - m_a))
pa <- profile_wsgn(ants, beta = c(2, 1, 0.5, 0.2, 0.05), window = 0.4)
pa$mu_degrees <- pa$mu * 180 / pi
pa[, c("beta", "logLik", "mu_degrees")]
#>   beta     logLik mu_degrees
#> 1 2.00 -147.79796   193.2992
#> 2 1.00 -135.41341   200.0000
#> 3 0.50 -125.09101   180.0000
#> 4 0.20  -91.24055   180.0000
#> 5 0.05  143.17113   180.0000

There are \(m = 17\) ants at 180 degrees, so \(A^* = 18.63\), and the profile rises monotonically as the shape decreases, with the location at the tied value. Any lower bound on \(\beta\) would be attained. When the data are rounded, the grouped likelihood \(\sum_j N_j\log\mathrm{P}(\Theta \in C_j)\) over the recording cells is the likelihood of what was observed. It is bounded with no constraint on the shape, and its cell probabilities follow exactly from the incomplete-gamma survival functions of the two sides. fit_wsgn() maximises it when resolution is given.

set.seed(4)
fit_g <- fit_wsgn(ants, resolution = pi / 18, se = "bootstrap", B = 10, ncores = 2)
fit_g
#> Wrapped two-piece exponential power distribution, maximum likelihood (grouped) 
#> n = 100  log-likelihood = -306.9329  shape floor = 0.001     
#>               mu   sigma     lam   beta
#> estimate 3.19200 0.08321 0.48180 0.5086
#> se       0.06337 0.05319 0.06574 0.0951
#> standard errors: bootstrap
coef(fit_g)["mu"] * 180 / pi
#>       mu 
#> 182.8971

The grouped maximum is interior, at \(\hat\beta = 0.51\) with the location at 182.9 degrees, and the 17 values at 180 degrees are simply a well-filled cell. As the shape tends to zero the family approaches mixtures of a point mass and the uniform distribution, and the best such mixture with its point mass in the modal cell reaches a grouped log-likelihood of -340.68, against -306.93 at the fit. The grouped likelihood-ratio statistic for \(\lambda = 1/2\) is 0.05, so these data show no evidence of skewness. Each bootstrap sample is rounded to the same cells before it is refitted.

Conclusion

The two-piece exponential power distribution and its wrapped version were treated as a uniform scale mixture with generalised gamma mixing, and the mixture was checked by simulation. The characteristic function and the trigonometric moments were computed on a rotated path of integration for every shape, and checked against the convergent series and the closed forms at shapes one and two, and the decay constant of the trigonometric moments was confirmed. The wrapped density, with its Euler-Maclaurin tail, integrated to one at every shape tried.

The four-parameter distribution was fitted to the Col de la Roa wind directions under a shape floor, with the location at an observation, and the profile likelihood showed the degenerate branch far below the floor. For the rounded ant directions the ordinary likelihood had no maximum, while the grouped likelihood gave an interior fit. The distribution has also been fitted to daily wind directions from the Namib Desert (SASSCAL WeatherNet). Their licence allows non-commercial use only, so they are not distributed with the package.

The computations of this vignette took 133 seconds.
